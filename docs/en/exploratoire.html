A. Deviation from trend (60 and 120 sessions)

Instead of the price direction, the model predicts the change in the price’s deviation from its 120-session moving average. The benchmark is the mean reversion predicted by the current deviation alone.

The current deviation alone predicts the direction of the change in 68 to 71% of cases, and the factors do worse. This high score is largely mechanical: the moving average catches up with the price with a lag, so a large deviation shrinks even if the price does not move. It is not a price forecast.

B. Volatility: do the factors add anything to the past?

The HAR model forecasts future volatility from past volatilities over 5, 20 and 60 sessions. The factors are then added to forecast what HAR misses.

Show the code

HAR clearly beats recent volatility alone at 5 and 20 sessions. Adding the factors worsens the forecast at all 4 horizons, with an entirely negative interval. QLIKE, another common error measure for volatility, gives the same verdict. For risk management, past volatility is enough.

C. In crisis periods, does attention matter?

The high-volatility regime is defined from the past only: volatility over the last 60 sessions exceeds the 80th percentile of its history. This covers 12.5% of the sessions in 2010-2023, against 95% of those in 2024-2026.

We measure the loss when the investors and biases family (Google Trends, GDELT news, CFTC positions) is removed, separately in each regime.

Show the code 
c = lire("pistes_regime.csv")
c = c[c["decoupe"].isin(["regime_haut", "regime_bas"])]
fig = go.Figure()
cibles = [("sens", h) for h in (1, 5, 20, 60, 120)] + [("vol", h) for h in (5, 20, 60, 120)]
etiq = {k: f"{'Dir.' if k[0] == 'sens' else 'Vol.'} {k[1]}" for k in cibles}
xpos = {k: i for i, k in enumerate(cibles)}
for j, (dec, nom) in enumerate([("regime_haut", "High volatility"), ("regime_bas", "Low volatility")]):
    x = c[c["decoupe"] == dec]
    fig.add_scatter(x=[xpos[(a, b)] + (j - 0.5) * 0.25 for a, b in zip(x["cible"], x["horizon"])], y=x["ecart"],
                    mode="markers", name=nom, marker=dict(size=9, color=SERIE[j], line=dict(width=2, color="white")),
                    error_y=dict(type="data", symmetric=False, array=x["ic_haut"] - x["ecart"],
                                 arrayminus=x["ecart"] - x["ic_bas"], color=SERIE[j], thickness=2, width=3),
                    customdata=np.stack([x["ic_bas"], x["ic_haut"]], axis=1),
                    hovertemplate=f"{nom}<br>Loss %{{y:+.4f}}<br>99.72% CI: %{{customdata[0]:+.4f}} to %{{customdata[1]:+.4f}}<extra></extra>")
fig.add_hline(y=0, line=dict(color=MUET, width=1.5, dash="dot"))
fig.update_layout(title="Loss when the investors and biases family is removed (positive = the family helps)", height=420,
                  xaxis=dict(tickvals=list(xpos.values()), ticktext=[etiq[k] for k in cibles]), yaxis_title="Score loss")
fig.show()

Only one case stands out: volatility at 60 sessions in high volatility, where the family helps the model (interval from +0.0004 to +0.0225). SHAP points to the volume of news articles on cocoa (GDELT), almost zero before 2024 and dominant afterwards. Four reasons for caution:

  • the effect is measured against the model without this family. The full model remains worse than past volatility alone;
  • media attention probably follows the crisis rather than announcing it;
  • the effect rests on about 11 independent windows;
  • the analysis was chosen after seeing the results.

No effect appears on the price direction, at any horizon.

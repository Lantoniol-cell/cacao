Method and limits

A protocol set before the results

All the rules were written in protocole.md and validated on 7 November 2026, before the first factor model: targets, horizons, settings, benchmark, success criterion, measure of family impact. The only analyses added afterwards are flagged as exploratory.

Item Choice
Targets Direction of the return over 1, 5, 20, 60 and 120 sessions; realised volatility over 5, 20, 60 and 120 sessions
Validation One test year at a time, from 2010 to 2026, training on the whole past
Purge h sessions between training and test, equal to the horizon
Models Gradient Boosting (main) and Random Forest (control), fixed settings, random_state=42
Benchmark Best model without factors: majority class, persistence, momentum, logistic regression on the past price; for volatility, persistence or past average
Success Beat the benchmark over 2010-2026 and in the control reading, with a 99.4% interval that excludes zero
Control reading Without contract roll windows up to 20 sessions; jump days neutralised at 60 and 120 sessions
Family impact Removing one family at a time (main), SHAP (secondary); no ranking if the benchmark is not beaten

Data and publication lags

A data point enters the model only once published, strictly before the session. This is the rule that prevents look-ahead bias.

Family Sources Lag applied
Market and finance Yahoo Finance (CC=F, dollar, rates, chocolate stocks), CFTC Previous close; CFTC on Friday
Climate ERA5 (daily rainfall), CHIRPS (monthly rainfall), NASA POWER (temperature, humidity, soil, radiation, wind), ONI ERA5 7 days; CHIRPS on the 25th of the following month; NASA 3 to 6 days; ONI about 45 days
Weather forecasts NOAA GEFS, days 2 to 10, corrected by country against CHIRPS Session after the run
Biodiversity FIRMS fires (NASA), FAOSTAT pesticides and nitrogen, FAOSTAT yield 1 day; FAOSTAT on 1 January of year N+2
Production and supply FAOSTAT production 1 January of year N+2
Production costs Oil, gas, FX (cedi, rupiah, naira, euro), fertilisers (World Bank) Previous close; monthly prices 5 days after month end
Investors and biases CFTC positions, Google Trends, GDELT news Trends 5 days after month end; GDELT 1 day
Geopolitics GPR index (Caldara and Iacoviello), GDELT events Daily GPR 7 days; GDELT 1 day
Demand and substitutes Sugar, palm oil, robusta coffee, chocolate stocks Same as the market

Weather data cover 349 points in cocoa areas, located with MapSPAM 2010. They are weighted within each country, then across countries by FAOSTAT production shares from year N-2.

Limits

  • Contract rolls. CC=F switches contracts: the price jumps, up to 30% in 2024, are detected after the fact. The control reading handles them, without removing them.
  • Few independent long-horizon observations. About 70 independent windows at 60 sessions and 35 at 120 sessions over 2010-2026.
  • Revised data. FAOSTAT is taken in its current vintage, not the one known at the time. The 2005-2019 GEFS reforecasts were produced after the fact.
  • Imperfect weather sources. NASA rainfall has a wet bias in 2020-2023, and ERA5 drifts against CHIRPS in West Africa. The GEFS temperature correction inherits the NASA bias.
  • Publication lags partly assumed (FIRMS, ONI, Google Trends).
  • Missing data. No stocks series: ICE certified stocks are paid, or available through Bloomberg. Nothing either on soil poisoning, swollen shoot or illegal gold mining. 2026 fires are missing.
  • Price only. The conclusion concerns forecasting price and volatility, not the causal effect of the factors on production.

Extensions

  • ICE certified stocks, to be exported from Bloomberg.
  • Corrected MapSPAM 2020 weights and ICCO weights.
  • Seasonal forecasts instead of 10-day forecasts.
  • Finer positioning data and options data for implied volatility.

Use of artificial intelligence

This project was carried out with Claude Code (Anthropic), which wrote the collection, cleaning and modelling scripts, as well as this site and its English translation. Every methodological choice was validated by hand before being applied: rainfall sources, protocol, handling of GEFS breaks, exploratory leads. Each step was recorded in a work log, which notes for each task what the AI did and how the result was checked. Rules imposed on the AI:

  • never invent data;
  • cross-check every dated event against a first-tier source;
  • use a data point only once published;
  • set the model rules before seeing the results.
